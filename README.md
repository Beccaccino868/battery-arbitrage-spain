# Spanish Day-Ahead Battery Arbitrage

Estimates the profit a small battery storage system could earn by charging on
cheap electricity and discharging when prices are high, using real Spanish
day-ahead market data.

## Data
Hourly PVPC prices for August 2026, from REE's public REData API
(apidatos.ree.es) — no authentication required.

## Method
Linear programming model (PuLP + CBC solver). For every hour, the model
decides how much to charge/discharge a 1 MW / 2 MWh battery (90% round-trip
efficiency) to maximize profit, subject to power and capacity limits.

A degradation cost (5 €/MWh cycled) is included in the objective, following
the common practice in the literature of penalizing cycling to avoid
unrealistically aggressive battery use (see e.g. Sandia's Electricity Storage
Handbook, ~2 $/MWh as a conservative O&M reference point).

## Results (August 2026, 696 hours)
- Profit, perfect-foresight, no degradation cost: €15,183.61
- Profit with degradation cost: €14,223.25 (-6.3%)
- Charging hours: 132/696 · Discharging hours: 90/696
  (vs. ~58/58 theoretical minimum for one clean cycle/day)
- Profit with degradation cost: €14,223.25
- Charging hours: 132/696 · Discharging hours: 90/696
  (vs. ~58/58 theoretical minimum for one clean cycle/day)

## Limitations
- Perfect foresight: the model knows all future prices; real operation would
  rely on forecasts, so actual profit would be lower
- No market/grid fees; degradation modeled as a flat cost per MWh cycled

## v2 — Solar self-consumption + battery

Extends v1 by adding a rooftop PV system and a residential consumption
profile, modeling not just grid arbitrage but self-consumption: the
battery now mediates between covering household demand directly,
storing free solar surplus, and classic price arbitrage.

### Data

- **PV production**: PVGIS (EU Joint Research Centre), hourly generation
  for a 2.2 kWp system, Valencia coordinates, historical year 2023
- **Prices**: REData, same source as v1 (PVPC for purchases, spot/PBF
  for excess sold back to the grid), January–June 2023
- **Household consumption**: synthetic hourly profile (not measured
  data), built from a standard bimodal residential load shape
  (morning + evening peaks) with simple seasonal adjustment, calibrated
  to ~3,000 kWh/year (average scenario) and ~5,500 kWh/year
  (average + EV charging scenario, +2,500 kWh/year)

### Method

Same linear programming approach as v1 (PuLP + CBC solver), extended
with three additional flows: the battery can now charge from solar
(free) as well as from the grid, and PV production is split each hour
between direct self-consumption, battery charging, and grid export.
Household demand is covered, in priority order the solver finds on its
own (not hardcoded), by direct solar, battery discharge, or grid
purchase.

Battery: 5 kW / 10 kWh, 90% round-trip efficiency, consistent with v1's
methodology but resized to residential scale (v1 modeled a 1 MW / 2 MWh
system for grid-scale arbitrage).

### Results (6-month period, average consumption scenario)

| Component | Value (€) |
|---|---|
| Self-consumption savings (direct PV + battery) | 249.81 |
| Direct PV surplus sold to grid | 8.60 |
| Battery arbitrage revenue (stored and resold at better prices) | 82.96 |
| Grid charging cost | −35.74 |
| Degradation cost | −17.90 |
| **Net benefit** | **287.73** |

Net benefit / baseline cost ratio: 215% (exceeds 100% because it
includes export and arbitrage revenue, not just avoided purchases —
see note below).

With EV charging added (+2,500 kWh/year), net benefit rises to €789.82
against a baseline cost of €457.98 (173% ratio).

The most notable result isn't self-consumption alone (which by itself
would already zero out the electricity bill) — it's the €82.96
generated by the battery's arbitrage behavior on top of that: storing
energy when it's cheap or free from solar, and selling when market
prices are higher. This shows the battery isn't just enabling
self-consumption, but continuing the price-arbitrage role already
demonstrated in v1, now integrated with solar generation.

### Limitations (in addition to v1's)

- **Perfect foresight** (inherited from v1) is especially visible here:
  grid purchases are zero in every single month of the simulated period,
  even January (276 kWh produced vs. 318 kWh consumed — a tight
  margin). A real system, without advance knowledge of future prices
  and production, would likely show residual grid purchases in winter
  months.
- Consumption profile is **synthetic**, based on a standard load shape,
  not measured data from an actual household.
- Excess energy compensation is approximated at the hourly spot price,
  not a specific retailer's contractual terms.
- EV charging is modeled as a flat annual kWh increase with the same
  hourly shape as the rest of consumption, not a realistic charging
  pattern (which typically concentrates in a few off-peak night hours).
  not a full electrochemical model
- Single market (Spain), single month sample
